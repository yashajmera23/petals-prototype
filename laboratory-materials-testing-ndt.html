Laboratory, Materials Testing & Non-Destructive Testing (NDT)

Testing and evaluation of soil, rock and construction materials, and non-destructive testing and condition assessment of concrete, structures and existing infrastructure.

One testing service, from the sample in the laboratory to the structure in use. Soil, rock and construction materials are tested to the applicable standards, and existing structures are assessed in place with non-destructive methods.

Soil, Rock & Construction Materials Testing

Laboratory tests on samples from site and on the materials that go into construction.

  • Soil

  • Natural Moisture Content

    Determines the water content of a soil sample as received, an input to classification and to most other test results.

  • Grain Size Analysis

    Sieve and hydrometer analysis to measure the proportions of gravel, sand, silt and clay in a soil.

  • Atterberg Limits

    Liquid and plastic limits that describe how a fine-grained soil behaves as its water content changes, used to classify clays and silts.

  • Specific Gravity

    Measures the density of soil solids relative to water, used in calculations of void ratio and degree of saturation.

  • Bulk Density & Dry Density

    Determines the bulk density and dry density of soil specimens by measuring their mass per unit volume and accounting for moisture content. In-situ density testing should be identified separately under field investigation where applicable.

  • Proctor Compaction Test

    Establishes the maximum dry density and optimum moisture content for compacting a soil, the reference for field compaction control.

  • California Bearing Ratio (CBR)

    Measures the resistance of compacted soil to penetration relative to a standard material, used in pavement design.

  • Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS)

    Loads a cylindrical cohesive soil specimen without lateral support to measure its compressive strength.

  • Direct Shear Test

    Shears a soil specimen along a fixed plane to determine its cohesion and angle of shearing resistance.

  • Triaxial Shear Test

    Tests a specimen under controlled confining pressure and drainage to measure shear strength parameters for design.

  • Consolidation Test

    Measures how a saturated clay compresses under increasing load and how fast, for settlement prediction.

  • Permeability Test

    Measures how readily water flows through a soil specimen, using constant-head or falling-head methods.

  • Free Swell Index & Swelling Pressure

    Measures the tendency of expansive clays to swell on wetting and the pressure they exert if restrained.

  • Chemical Tests on Soil

    Determines pH, sulphates, chlorides and organic content that can affect foundations, concrete and steel.

  • Rock

  • Uniaxial Compressive Strength (UCS)

    Loads a rock core to failure to measure its compressive strength, a key input for rock foundation design.

  • Point Load Strength Index

    A quick test that breaks a core or rock lump between two points to index its strength.

  • Density & Specific Gravity

    Determines the density and specific gravity of rock specimens for classification and design.

  • Water Absorption & Porosity

    Measures how much water rock absorbs and its pore volume, indicators of weathering and durability.

  • Slake Durability Test

    Measures the resistance of weak or clay-bearing rock to breaking down through wetting and drying cycles.

  • Triaxial Rock Testing

    Tests rock cores under confining pressure to determine strength parameters for design.

  • Cement

  • Fineness of Cement

    Measures the particle fineness of cement, which affects the rate of strength gain.

  • Standard Consistency

    Determines the water content that gives cement paste a standard consistency, the basis for other cement tests.

  • Setting Time

    Measures the initial and final setting times of cement paste.

  • Soundness

    Checks that cement does not expand excessively after setting, which could crack concrete.

  • Compressive Strength of Cement

    Tests mortar cubes at specified ages to confirm the strength grade of cement.

  • Concrete

  • Compressive Strength

    Tests concrete cubes or cylinders at specified ages to confirm the specified strength is achieved.

  • Flexural Strength

    Tests concrete beams in bending, used mainly for pavement concrete.

  • Split Tensile Strength

    Loads a concrete cylinder along its length to measure indirect tensile strength.

  • Concrete Mix Design Support

    Proportions cement, aggregates, water and admixtures to achieve the required strength, workability and durability.

  • Core Testing

    Tests cores cut from hardened concrete to assess the in-place strength of existing structures.

  • Aggregates

  • Sieve Analysis

    Determines the particle size distribution of aggregates against grading requirements.

  • Aggregate Crushing Value

    Measures the resistance of aggregate to crushing under a gradually applied load.

  • Aggregate Impact Value

    Measures the resistance of aggregate to sudden impact.

  • Los Angeles Abrasion

    Measures the resistance of coarse aggregate to abrasion and impact in a rotating drum.

  • Flakiness & Elongation Index

    Measures the proportion of flat and elongated particles, which affect strength and workability.

  • Water Absorption & Specific Gravity

    Determines aggregate density and water absorption for mix design.

  • Soundness Test

    Measures the resistance of aggregate to weathering through repeated sulphate solution cycles.

  • Steel

  • Tensile Test

    Measures the yield strength, ultimate strength and elongation of reinforcement and structural steel.

  • Bend & Rebend Test

    Bends reinforcement bars through specified angles to check ductility and freedom from cracking.

  • Bitumen & Asphalt

  • Penetration Test

    Measures the consistency of bitumen by the depth a standard needle penetrates under set conditions.

  • Softening Point

    Determines the temperature at which bitumen softens under standard test conditions.

  • Ductility Test

    Measures the distance a bitumen specimen stretches before breaking.

  • Viscosity Test

    Measures the flow resistance of bitumen at specified temperatures, used to grade binders.

  • Bitumen Content

    Determines the binder content of an asphalt mix by extraction.

  • Aggregate Gradation of Asphalt Mix

    Checks the grading of aggregates recovered from an asphalt mix against the specified limits.

  • Marshall Stability & Flow

    Measures the stability and deformation of compacted asphalt specimens, used in mix design and quality control.

  • Other Materials

  • Water Testing for Construction

    Tests water for pH, chlorides, sulphates and solids to confirm it is suitable for mixing and curing concrete.

  • Material-Specific Testing

    Testing of other construction materials such as bricks, blocks and tiles to the applicable standards.

Non-Destructive Testing

Testing concrete in place, without damaging the structure.

  • Rebound Hammer Test

    Measures the surface rebound hardness of concrete to assess relative uniformity and identify variations in surface quality. Any estimate of compressive strength should be based on an appropriate project-specific correlation with core test results and not solely on generic rebound values.

  • Ultrasonic Pulse Velocity (UPV)

    Measures the travel time and velocity of ultrasonic pulses through concrete to assess its quality and uniformity. Variations in pulse velocity may indicate possible cracks, voids or other anomalies, which should be interpreted considering the test arrangement and other investigation findings.

  • Concrete Cover Meter Test

    Locates reinforcement and measures the depth of concrete cover over it.

  • Half-Cell Potential Test

    Measures electrical potentials on the concrete surface to indicate the likelihood of reinforcement corrosion.

  • Carbonation Depth Test

    Measures how far carbonation has penetrated the concrete, which affects protection of the reinforcement.

  • Impact-Echo Testing

    Uses stress waves from a small impact to detect voids, delamination and thickness in concrete elements.

  • Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR)

    Uses radar reflections to locate and map detectable reinforcement, embedded services and possible internal anomalies within concrete elements. Detection and interpretation depend on concrete conditions, reinforcement arrangement, cover depth and equipment limitations.

  • Infrared Thermography

    Uses thermal imaging to identify surface temperature patterns and anomalies that may indicate delamination, moisture ingress or other potential defects. Findings require interpretation and, where necessary, confirmation through suitable inspection or testing.

5

Structural Condition Assessment

Recording the condition of existing structures and reporting what it means. Core cutting is destructive testing, used to confirm what non-destructive methods indicate.

  • Visual Condition Survey

    Systematic inspection and recording of visible defects such as cracking, spalling, corrosion staining and leakage.

  • Crack Mapping

    Records the location, width, length and pattern of cracks to help identify their cause and monitor change.

  • Structural Steel Visual Inspection

    Inspects steel members and connections for corrosion, distortion and defects.

  • Condition Assessment Reporting

    Brings test results and observations together into a report on structural condition and recommended next steps.

  • Concrete & Bitumen Core Cutting & Testing

    Plans suitable core locations, extraction and laboratory testing to assess the in-situ properties and compressive strength of hardened concrete.

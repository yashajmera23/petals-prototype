How We Work

A structured approach to understanding requirements, executing technical work and delivering clear outputs.

Whether a project needs ground investigation, testing, design or all three, it moves through the same six stages, and the four working principles below hold at every one of them.

  1. 01Understand
  2. 02Define the Scope
  3. 03Plan & Mobilise
  4. 04Execute
  5. 05Review & Interpret
  6. 06Report & Deliver

Six stages, every project

Each stage has a clear output before the next begins, so scope, programme and deliverables stay visible from the first conversation to the final report.

  1. 1

    Understand

    Review the project objectives, site information and client requirements.

  2. 2

    Define the Scope

    Identify required services, methods, deliverables and technical inputs.

  3. 3

    Plan & Mobilise

    Coordinate resources, equipment, schedules, access and site requirements.

  4. 4

    Execute

    Carry out agreed investigation, testing, analysis or engineering activities.

  5. 5

    Review & Interpret

    Check available data, evaluate findings and address technical discrepancies.

  6. 6

    Report & Deliver

    Submit agreed outputs and communicate relevant findings and limitations.

One stream or both

At the Execute stage the work runs in up to two connected streams. Try each scope to see how a project uses them.

Two connected workstreams

Depending on scope, a project may use one stream or both, and they share information throughout.

Investigate & Test

Geotechnical investigation, geophysics, laboratory testing, NDT and foundation testing.

Engineer & Design

Engineering analysis, design development, BIM and digital coordination.

Working principles

Four commitments that apply at every stage, on every project.

  • Technical Accuracy

    Appropriate methods, reliable records and careful review.

  • Clear Communication

    Defined expectations, progress updates and prompt clarification.

  • Planned Delivery

    Timelines aligned with scope, access and resource availability.

  • Defined Deliverables

    Outputs agreed at the outset and prepared for their intended use.

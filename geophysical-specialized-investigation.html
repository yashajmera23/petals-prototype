Geophysical & Specialized Investigation

Geophysical surveys, subsurface characterisation, surveying and hydrogeological investigations.

Geophysics reads the ground between boreholes. We combine non-invasive surveys with targeted drilling, map buried services before excavation, and survey and study groundwater so the site is understood as a whole.

What we cover

10

Geophysical Investigation

Non-invasive methods used to investigate and characterise subsurface conditions along survey lines or across defined areas, complementing direct borehole investigation. Survey coverage, depth and resolution depend on the selected method and ground conditions.

  • Seismic Refraction

    Measures the travel times of seismic waves refracted along layer boundaries to map depth to bedrock and changes in ground stiffness.

  • MASW

    Multichannel Analysis of Surface Waves: derives a shear-wave velocity profile with depth from surface waves recorded on a line of geophones.

  • SASW

    Spectral Analysis of Surface Waves: uses a pair of receivers to estimate the shear-wave velocity profile at a test location.

  • ReMi

    Refraction Microtremor: uses ambient vibration rather than an active source to estimate shear-wave velocity, useful on busy or noisy sites.

  • Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT)

    Builds a two-dimensional image of subsurface resistivity variations to identify changes in soil and rock conditions, moisture distribution, weathered or fractured zones and possible voids. Results require geological interpretation and, where necessary, correlation with direct investigation.

  • Vertical Electrical Sounding (VES)

    Measures resistivity with increasing electrode spacing at one point to interpret layering with depth, often for groundwater and earthing studies.

  • Borehole Seismic Surveys

    Downhole and cross-hole seismic tests that measure wave velocities directly in boreholes for dynamic and seismic design parameters.

  • Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR)

    Transmits radar pulses into the ground and records reflections to investigate shallow subsurface interfaces, buried objects, utilities and possible voids. Detection depth and resolution depend on ground conductivity, target size, material contrast, antenna frequency and site conditions.

  • Gravity & Magnetic Surveys

    Measures variations in gravity or magnetic fields to identify subsurface anomalies associated with changes in density, magnetic properties and geological structures. Anomalies require geological interpretation and may need verification through complementary investigation.

  • Induced Polarisation (IP)

    Measures how the ground stores and releases electrical charge, helping distinguish clay-rich layers and mineralised zones from other materials.

3

Utility & Subsurface Detection

Finding what is buried before excavation or drilling begins.

  • Underground Utility Detection

    Uses suitable electromagnetic locating equipment and/or GPR to detect, trace and mark identifiable underground utilities before excavation or drilling. Detection depends on utility material, depth, accessibility, surrounding ground conditions and possible signal interference.

  • Buried Object & Void Detection

    Uses suitable geophysical and subsurface detection methods to identify anomalies that may indicate buried foundations, tanks, cavities or voids. Findings should be interpreted in the context of site conditions and verified through targeted investigation wherever required.

  • Subsurface Mapping

    Combines survey results into plans and sections of buried features and ground conditions for design and construction planning.

4

Surveying

The site measured and set out, from levels and contours to long route corridors.

  • Topographical Survey

    Records ground levels, features and boundaries of a site to produce a base drawing for planning and design.

  • Contour & Site Level Survey

    Captures ground levels on a grid to produce contour plans for grading, drainage and earthwork calculations.

  • Engineering & Setting-Out Survey

    Transfers design coordinates to the ground, setting out structures, piles and alignments for construction.

  • Route & Corridor Survey

    Surveys long linear corridors for roads, pipelines and transmission lines to support alignment selection and design.

5

Hydrogeological Investigation

Where groundwater is, how much there is and how it behaves. Dewatering is not included.

  • Groundwater Exploration

    Uses geological, geophysical and field information to identify locations and depths with potential for groundwater.

  • Aquifer Characterisation

    Defines the extent, thickness and properties of water-bearing strata from borehole, test and survey data.

  • Pumping & Yield Tests

    Pumps a well at a controlled rate while recording water levels to estimate yield and aquifer properties.

  • Groundwater Level Monitoring

    Records groundwater levels over time in wells or piezometers to capture seasonal changes and long-term trends.

  • Hydrogeological Mapping

    Maps aquifers, water levels and flow directions across an area to support planning and water resource decisions.

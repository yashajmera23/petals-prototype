Foundation & Pile Testing

Load testing and integrity assessment of foundations and piles.

Piles are hidden once they are cast. Load tests confirm what they can carry, and integrity tests confirm they were built as designed, so the structure above starts on proven foundations.

What we cover

16 parameters in 4 groups. Select any item to see what it involves. Specialist tests are delivered through PETALS' technical network and qualified partners where required.

6

Static Load Testing

Loading a pile and measuring how it responds, to confirm capacity.

  • Initial Pile Load Test

    Tests a designated test pile by applying specified load increments and recording its load–settlement response to assess pile behaviour and verify design assumptions. Test loads and acceptance criteria are determined by the applicable code, project specifications and testing requirements.

  • Routine Pile Load Test

    Tests selected working piles to confirm they meet the design requirements.

  • Pile Compression Load Test

    Applies axial compressive load in stages and records settlement to assess load–settlement behaviour.

  • Pile Tension / Pull-Out Test

    Applies an upward load to assess the uplift resistance of piles under tension.

  • Pile Lateral Load Test

    Applies horizontal load at the pile head and records deflection to assess lateral capacity.

  • Bi-Directional Pile Load Test

    Loads the pile from within using an embedded jack, testing the shaft and base separately without a reaction frame above ground.

2

Dynamic Pile Testing

Faster capacity checks from the pile’s response to an impact.

  • High-Strain Dynamic Load Test

    Strikes the pile head with a heavy mass and analyses the response to estimate capacity.

  • Pile Driving Analyser (PDA) Monitoring

    Uses instrumentation to record pile strain and acceleration during pile driving or high-strain dynamic testing. The recorded data are analysed to evaluate dynamic pile response, estimated capacity, pile stresses and hammer performance.

4

Pile Integrity Testing

Assessing pile continuity and identifying significant changes in cross-section or possible anomalies along the pile, subject to test method, access conditions, pile geometry and interpretation limitations.

  • Pile Integrity Test (PIT)

    A low-strain impact test that analyses reflected stress waves to assess pile continuity and identify significant changes in cross-section or possible defects. Pile length may be estimated where a clear toe reflection is obtained and test conditions permit reliable interpretation.

  • Cross-Hole Sonic Logging (CSL)

    Measures ultrasonic signals between tubes cast into the pile to locate defects in the concrete.

  • Parallel Seismic Testing

    Uses receivers installed in a nearby borehole to investigate the length of existing or inaccessible piles where direct pile-head testing is not feasible. Results depend on borehole location, pile geometry, test arrangement and interpretation.

  • Thermal Integrity Profiling

    Uses temperature measurements during concrete curing, generally obtained through sensors installed in the pile reinforcement cage, to assess pile shape, concrete cover variations and possible anomalies. Interpretation depends on sensor arrangement, concrete placement and test conditions.

4

Instrumentation & Reporting

Measuring what happens inside the pile, and reporting what it means.

  • Instrumented Pile Testing

    Uses strain gauges and extensometers in the pile to measure how load is shared between shaft and base.

  • Pile Settlement & Displacement Monitoring

    Records pile head movement during and after testing with dial gauges, transducers or survey.

  • Load-Settlement Curve & Analysis

    Plots and interprets the pile load–settlement response to assess pile behaviour and compare the test results with design requirements. Allowable or safe load recommendations are derived using the applicable code, test procedure and project-specific acceptance criteria.

  • Foundation Testing Report

    Brings test data, analysis and conclusions together in a report for the design team.

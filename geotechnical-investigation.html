Geotechnical Investigation

Subsurface investigation, field testing, soil and rock evaluation, and geotechnical engineering and design.

Every foundation decision starts with the ground. We plan each investigation around the structure and the site, test and log what is there, and turn the results into clear design recommendations.

Field Investigation & Testing

Tests carried out on site, in boreholes, pits and at foundation level.

  • Borehole Drilling

    Advance boreholes to specified depths to investigate subsurface strata and facilitate soil/rock sampling and in-situ testing.

  • Standard Penetration Test (SPT)

    Records the blows required to drive a standard split-spoon sampler over the specified penetration interval. The N-value is an index of penetration resistance and should be interpreted using applicable corrections, such as energy and overburden corrections where relevant, along with correlations appropriate to the soil type and ground conditions. It is not a direct measurement of soil density or strength.

  • Cone Penetration Test (CPT/CPTu)

    Measures cone resistance and sleeve friction during continuous penetration into the ground. CPTu additionally measures pore-water pressure, providing further information for subsurface soil characterisation.

  • Dynamic Cone Penetration Test (DCPT)

    Uses a dynamically driven cone to assess soil penetration resistance through the number of blows required for specified penetration. Commonly used for shallow soil and pavement subgrade investigations.

  • Trial Pits / Test Pits

    Shallow excavations that enable direct examination of soil strata, collection of samples and observation of groundwater where encountered.

  • Field Vane Shear Test

    Measures the torque required to rotate a vane in suitable soft cohesive soils to estimate their in-situ undrained shear strength.

  • Plate Load Test

    Applies incremental loading to a steel plate and measures the corresponding load–settlement response to assess the bearing behaviour and deformation characteristics of the ground at the test level. Results represent the conditions at the test location and depth and must be interpreted considering plate size, test conditions and applicable design criteria. They should not be treated as a universal measure of allowable bearing capacity.

  • Field CBR Test

    Determines the in-situ California Bearing Ratio (CBR) of soil or pavement subgrade under specified test conditions for pavement evaluation and design inputs.

  • In-situ Permeability Testing

    Evaluates the hydraulic conductivity of soil or rock using suitable field permeability testing methods in boreholes, pits or other designated test locations.

  • Pressuremeter Testing

    Expands a cylindrical probe against the borehole wall and measures the pressure–deformation response of the surrounding ground to derive relevant geotechnical parameters.

6

Sampling, Logging & Groundwater

What is recovered from the ground, how it is recorded, and what the water table is doing.

  • Disturbed Soil Sampling

    Collection of representative soil samples for classification, identification and laboratory testing where preservation of the natural soil structure is not essential.

  • Undisturbed Soil Sampling

    Recovery of relatively undisturbed soil specimens, preserving their natural structure and moisture content as far as practicable, for appropriate strength, consolidation and other laboratory tests.

  • Rock Coring & Core Logging

    Recovery of rock cores followed by systematic logging of lithology, weathering, discontinuities, core recovery and other relevant rock characteristics.

  • Borehole Logging & Strata Profiling

    Recording encountered soil and rock strata, their depths, sampling details, in-situ test results and relevant drilling observations to develop a representative subsurface profile.

  • Rock Quality Designation (RQD)

    Calculates the percentage of a core run comprising qualifying sound rock pieces of 100 mm or longer relative to the total core run length, providing an index of rock-mass quality.

  • Groundwater Level Observation

    Records groundwater encountered during investigation and subsequent groundwater-level observations after specified stabilisation periods to support geotechnical assessment and design.

Geotechnical Engineering & Design

Engineering design and analysis, not only investigation: turning the findings into foundation, slope and retaining-structure decisions.

  • Geotechnical Design Recommendations

    Develops geotechnical design recommendations covering suitable foundation options, founding levels and relevant design parameters based on investigation findings, structural requirements and project-specific design criteria.

  • Bearing Capacity & Settlement Analysis

    Evaluates foundation bearing resistance and estimates anticipated settlement under defined loading, foundation geometry and subsurface conditions.

  • Shallow Foundation Design

    Provides geotechnical design support for isolated, strip, combined and raft foundations considering soil conditions, structural loading and applicable design criteria.

  • Pile Foundation Design Support

    Provides geotechnical design inputs for pile selection, estimated axial and lateral capacity, settlement assessment and foundation design considerations.

  • Earth Pressure & Retaining Structures

    Evaluates lateral earth pressures and relevant ground conditions for the design of retaining walls, basement structures and bridge abutments, considering applicable loading and groundwater conditions.

  • Slope & Embankment Stability

    Evaluates the stability of natural slopes, cuttings and embankments under defined soil, groundwater, geometric and loading conditions.

  • Ground Improvement Assessment

    Assesses ground improvement requirements and evaluates suitable improvement methods based on subsurface conditions, project objectives and engineering constraints.

  • Excavation Stability Assessment

    Assesses excavation stability, temporary support requirements, groundwater considerations and potential effects on adjacent ground and structures.

  • Liquefaction Assessment

    Evaluates the susceptibility of soil deposits to liquefaction under earthquake loading, considering subsurface conditions, groundwater levels and applicable seismic demand.

  • Soil–Structure Interaction

    Evaluates the interaction between soil, foundations and structural systems to understand load transfer, deformation and their influence on overall engineering behaviour.
